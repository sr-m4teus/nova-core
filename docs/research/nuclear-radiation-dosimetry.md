# Radiation Dosimetry Research — Grounding for NovaCore's Nuclear Physics Pillar

Purpose: reference facts for designing a physically-grounded player radiation exposure mechanic. Pure research, no implementation decisions made here.

## 1. Dose units and reference scales

**Units.** The SI unit of *absorbed dose* is the gray (Gy) = 1 joule of energy deposited per kilogram of matter. The SI unit of *equivalent/effective dose* — the biologically-weighted quantity that actually predicts health risk — is the sievert (Sv). For gamma rays, X-rays, and beta particles, the radiation "quality factor" is 1, so 1 Gy ≈ 1 Sv; for alpha particles the factor is ~20 and for neutrons 5–20, meaning the same absorbed energy is far more damaging internally. The pre-SI units are rad (1 rad = 0.01 Gy) and rem (1 rem = 0.01 Sv) — exact by definition (100 rad/Gy, 100 rem/Sv). **Recommendation: standardize on Sv/mSv/µSv throughout the mod**, since it's the unit that maps to biological effect, and use mSv for all in-game UI numbers (whole Sv values are rare outside disaster scenarios). ([NRC 10 CFR 20.1004](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/cfr/part020/part020-1004), [Sievert – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sievert))

**Reference dose levels (real world):**
- Average US natural background: ~3 mSv/year (dominated by radon, plus cosmic and terrestrial sources); global average is closer to 2.4 mSv/year. ([NRC – Doses in Our Daily Lives](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/radiation/around-us/doses-daily-lives))
- Chest X-ray: ~0.1 mSv (≈10 days of background). ([UCSF Know Your Dose](https://knowyourdose.ucsf.edu/how-much-radiation))
- CT scans: highly variable by body region — head CT ~2 mSv, chest CT ~7 mSv, i.e. multiple months to a couple of years of background dose in one scan. ([How Radiology Works — X-ray/CT dose comparisons](https://howradiologyworks.com/x-ray-ct-dose-to-background/))
- ICRP/NRC occupational limit: 20 mSv/year averaged over 5 years, no single year over 50 mSv — this is a regulatory limit, not an injury threshold.
- Acute Radiation Syndrome (ARS) onset threshold: roughly 0.7–1 Sv delivered acutely (whole-body, short timeframe) before measurable clinical symptoms appear; CDC frames the practical threshold as >0.7 Gy. ([CDC — ARS Clinical Guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/hcp/clinical-guidance/ars.html))
- Hematopoietic (bone-marrow) syndrome: >2–3 Gy. Gastrointestinal syndrome: 5–12 Gy. Cerebrovascular/CNS syndrome: 10–20 Gy, essentially unsurvivable.
- LD50/60 (dose lethal to 50% of victims within 60 days) **without treatment**: ~4 Sv acute whole-body. With modern intensive medical care (transfusions, antibiotics, isolation, marrow transplant) this rises to roughly 6–9 Gy. Doses above ~10–12 Gy are not survivable by any known treatment. ([PNNL-14424 — Health Impacts from Acute Radiation Exposure](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/pnnl-14424.pdf))

The key design takeaway: real doses span *many orders of magnitude* (µSv for daily background, mSv for medical/occupational, Sv for acute injury) — any in-game scale needs either a log-like curve or distinct "safe / caution / danger / lethal" bands rather than one linear bar.

## 2. Shielding and protection that actually works

**Distance — inverse-square law.** For a point-like source, dose rate falls off as 1/r². Doubling distance from a source cuts dose rate to a quarter. This is real, exact physics (for an unshielded point source in air) and is one of the cheapest, most game-friendly mechanics to model directly.

**Time.** Total dose = dose rate × exposure time. Simply spending less time near a source linearly reduces accumulated dose — the standard "time, distance, shielding" triad taught in health physics.

**Shielding — half-value layer (HVL).** Attenuation through a material follows an exponential law, I = I₀·e^(−μx); the HVL is the thickness that cuts intensity by half, and the tenth-value layer (TVL) cuts it to 10%. Lead is the standard practical shielding material because its high density gives a short HVL for a given thickness: e.g., for Cobalt-60 gammas, roughly ½ inch (~1.3 cm) of lead halves the dose rate, and ~1.5 inches reduces it by a factor of 10. HVL is strongly energy-dependent — higher-energy gammas need thicker shielding — which is realistic material for tiering in-game shielding blocks by radiation "hardness." ([Half-Value Layer / attenuation overview](https://marshield.com/gamma-ray-attenuation-properties-of-common-shielding-materials/), [Lead Thickness for Radiation Protection](https://barriertechnologies.com/lead-thickness-for-radiation-protection/))

**Hazmat/radiation suits — the important nuance.** Standard hazmat/CBRN suits are essentially useless against gamma or neutron radiation penetrating from outside the body — gamma photons pass through fabric, rubber, and even thin metal with little attenuation; only thick dense mass (lead, concrete, water, steel) meaningfully blocks them. What hazmat suits *actually* do is prevent **contamination**: they keep alpha-emitting dust and beta-emitting fallout particles off skin and clothing, and (combined with a respirator) keep radioactive particulates out of the lungs and digestive tract. Alpha particles can't even penetrate intact skin — the danger is inhalation/ingestion, not skin dose. So a suit dramatically cuts *internal* contamination dose but does essentially nothing for *external gamma/neutron* dose, which must instead be managed by time, distance, and mass shielding. This is a common inaccuracy in games (suits treated as generic "radiation armor" that flatly reduces all radiation damage). ([MIRA Safety / hazmat suit protection scope](https://www.mirasafety.com/products/mira-safety-haz-suit-hazmat-suit), [PK Safety — radiation suits](https://pksafety.com/blogs/pk-safety-blog/do-you-need-a-hazmat-suit-for-radiation))

## 3. How detection actually works

Geiger-Müller counters and ionization chambers are both gaseous ionization detectors: incoming radiation ionizes gas inside a sealed tube, freeing electrons that are pulled toward a charged central electrode. In a Geiger-Müller tube, the electrode voltage (~900 V) is high enough that the initial ionization triggers a cascade ("secondary ionization"/gas amplification), producing one loud, uniform pulse (a "click") per particle detected — this makes it a simple, cheap event counter but poor at measuring dose *rate* precisely, since it can't easily distinguish particle energy. An ionization chamber runs at lower voltage without gas amplification and instead measures a continuous current proportional to ionization rate, making it better suited to true dose-rate measurement (this is what most electronic dosimeters use internally). A personal dosimeter integrates detected ionization events over time to track *accumulated* dose for a wearer, distinct from a Geiger counter's instantaneous rate readout. Conceptually: **ionizing radiation strips electrons off gas atoms; counting or measuring that ionization is the entire basis of detection** — this maps cleanly onto an in-game detector item that "counts clicks" near radioactive blocks. ([Geiger Counter vs Ionization Chamber](https://www.nuclear-power.com/nuclear-engineering/radiation-detection/gaseous-ionization-detector/geiger-counter-vs-ionization-chamber/), [How Geiger Counters Work](https://www.ntanet.net/how-do-geiger-counters-work))

## 4. Candidate design ideas (not a decision — for later discussion)

- **Dose-rate via inverse-square law**: each radioactive block/entity emits a "source strength" (µSv/h at 1 block distance); actual dose rate the player receives = source strength / distance², summed across all sources in range, sampled each tick/second. Grounded directly in real physics and cheap to compute.
- **Accumulated dose ledger per player**: track lifetime (or rolling-window, e.g. per Minecraft day) absorbed dose in mSv, persisted in player data, decaying slowly to model biological repair of sub-lethal damage over time (real bodies do repair minor DNA damage between exposures) rather than being purely permanent.
- **Staged effects at real threshold fractions**: no effect below a "background-equivalent" band; mild debuffs (nausea/hunger, mirroring early ARS prodromal symptoms) starting near the real ~0.7–1 Sv acute threshold; severe effects (health drain, mirroring hematopoietic syndrome) past ~2–3 Sv; near-certain death scaled toward the ~4 Sv unaided LD50 and ~10+ Sv unsurvivable range — giving the player a legible, real-world-anchored ramp instead of an arbitrary bar.
- **Separate "external dose" vs "contamination/internal dose" tracks**: external gamma dose reduced only by distance/time/mass shielding (blocks, lead aprons, terrain), while a second contamination mechanic (dust/fallout picked up from broken radioactive blocks, contaminated water, etc.) is what a hazmat suit and respirator actually mitigate — this preserves the real nuance that suits ≠ gamma shielding.
- **Detector item as an ionization-event counter**: an in-game Geiger counter reads instantaneous local dose rate (clicks/sound scale with nearby source strength via the same inverse-square formula), while a separate dosimeter item integrates and displays the player's accumulated dose — mirroring the real distinction between rate-meters and integrating dosimeters, and giving the player actionable, diegetic feedback without a HUD number.

**Honest flag for design discussion:** if NovaCore wants full accuracy, hazmat gear (its own future armor set) should be modeled as reducing the *contamination* track heavily but the *external gamma* track little-to-not-at-all; only mass/thickness-based shielding blocks (and distance/time) should meaningfully cut external dose. Treating hazmat armor as a flat "radiation resistance %" against all radiation sources would repeat the common game inaccuracy noted in §2.
